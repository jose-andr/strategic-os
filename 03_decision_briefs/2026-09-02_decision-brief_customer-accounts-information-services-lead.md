# Decision Brief — Customer Accounts and Information Services Lead

## Brief Details

Date: 2026-09-02

Decision owner: To confirm

## Decision Required

### Decision required

Should the existing Customer Focus Project Officer role be evolved into a Class/Band 6 Customer Accounts and Information Services Lead role to establish an enduring internal capability for customer account quality, service information quality and connected-interactions readiness?

### Decision type

Choose between options / Proceed

### Timing

FY2026/27 planning and capability development.

### Why now

Current CX priorities increasingly depend on reliable customer accounts, consistent service information, clearer information architecture and stronger coordination across digital, CRM, contact centre, knowledge and service-delivery pathways.

These capabilities are beginning to operate as an interconnected service need rather than isolated project activities.

A decision is required on whether to formalise ownership now or continue managing these activities through existing project and officer arrangements.

---

# Recommendation

## Recommended course of action

Progress the amendment of the existing Project Officer Customer Focus role from Class/Band 5 to Class/Band 6 as the preferred pathway for establishing a Customer Accounts and Information Services Lead capability, subject to organisational classification, workforce and governance review.

Treat creation of a separate new role as the fallback organisational pathway rather than the starting assumption.

## Recommendation Summary

Formalising the capability through an evolved existing role appears to provide the clearest connection between current CX priorities and the operational foundations required for connected customer interactions.

The case should be positioned primarily as an **organisational capability and service-ownership decision**, not as a role-upgrade request.

## What This Enables

This enables:

- clearer ownership of customer account and service information quality;
- coordinated direction for the Information Officer and Customer Data Officer;
- stronger integration between account quality, knowledge quality and information architecture;
- a managed improvement roadmap rather than disconnected tasks;
- practical governance translated into day-to-day service standards;
- stronger foundations for CRM, digital, knowledge, AI and connected-channel initiatives;
- continuity between current operational improvement and future customer-interaction transformation.

## What This Does Not Decide

This decision does not by itself determine:

- formal position classification;
- workforce approval;
- final reporting arrangements;
- final role title;
- detailed operating procedures;
- technology ownership;
- enterprise data-governance ownership;
- funding decisions outside the role arrangement; or
- the implementation of future omnichannel technology.

Those remain subject to the appropriate organisational processes and decision owners.

---

# Why It Matters

## Customer Or Public Value

The proposed capability is intended to strengthen the information foundations that support clearer pathways, reduced repetition, more useful status information and more consistent support across customer channels.

The customer benefit is indirect but important: connected interactions depend on reliable account, service and handover information.

## Organisational Value

Formal ownership could reduce fragmentation between customer account quality, service information quality and related continuous-improvement activity.

It could also give the organisation:

- clearer accountability;
- an integrated improvement roadmap;
- more consistent standards;
- coordinated quality controls;
- better root-cause analysis; and
- a defined internal service offering.

## Strategic Value

The proposal directly supports the shift from improving individual customer channels toward designing connected customer interactions.

It also aligns with FY2026/27 Customer Focus and Strategy priorities relating to:

- customer strategy;
- CRM adoption;
- digital customer experience;
- information architecture;
- customer account management;
- knowledge-centred service; and
- stronger customer and business information foundations.

The strategic opportunity is therefore broader than the role itself.

The role could become a practical mechanism for connecting several existing CX priorities around a common information and account-quality capability.

## Risk Of No Decision

If ownership remains informal:

- customer account quality may continue as task-based remediation;
- service information quality may remain distributed across teams;
- related roles may produce useful outputs without a single improvement direction;
- connected-interaction work may become overly technology-led;
- governance principles may not translate into operational practice;
- recurring quality issues may continue to be treated individually rather than at source.

The primary risk is fragmentation rather than immediate service failure.

# Context

## Background

The Customer Focus and Strategy team is moving from individual channel improvement toward a more connected model of customer interaction.

Current work includes Channel Strategy Y2, CRM adoption, Service Account improvements, information architecture, customer account management and knowledge-centred service.

These initiatives increasingly rely on shared information foundations.

A proposal has therefore emerged to evolve the existing Project Officer Customer Focus role into a Customer Accounts and Information Services Lead.

## Current State

Available working material indicates that:

- customer account quality activity already exists;
- service information and knowledge-quality activity already exists;
- information architecture has become an explicit CX priority;
- Channel Strategy Y2 requires reusable service logic across channels;
- the Information Officer and Customer Data Officer represent existing capability that could be coordinated;
- current CX planning explicitly identifies customer account management, information architecture and knowledge-centred service as priority activities.

The material does not yet establish that the proposed organisational model, classification or reporting arrangement has been formally validated.

## Problem Or Opportunity

The central issue is not whether customer data and service information matter.

The strategic question is whether these related activities now require **explicit service ownership and integrated leadership**.

The opportunity is to convert several related operational activities into a coherent CX capability that supports both current service quality and future connected interactions.

## Scope

### In scope

- customer account quality;
- service information quality;
- service information architecture;
- improvement prioritisation;
- quality standards;
- practical governance patterns;
- coordination of relevant officer roles;
- readiness foundations for connected interactions.

### Out of scope

- ownership of enterprise CRM platforms;
- ownership of enterprise data governance;
- ownership of all organisational knowledge;
- replacement of service owners;
- ownership of every customer channel;
- detailed workforce-classification assessment;
- implementation of omnichannel technology.

# Options Considered

## Option A — Evolve the existing role

### Description

Amend the existing Project Officer Customer Focus role from Class/Band 5 to Class/Band 6 and establish the Customer Accounts and Information Services Lead capability.

### Benefits

- builds from existing capability;
- creates clearer ownership;
- integrates related officer roles;
- provides continuity with current work;
- creates an enduring improvement roadmap;
- potentially lower implementation friction than establishing a new function from scratch.

### Costs or effort

- position-description redesign;
- classification and workforce processes;
- clarification of decision rights;
- management responsibilities;
- establishment of service measures and operating routines.

### Risks

- role may become too broad;
- boundaries with enterprise data, CRM and knowledge ownership may remain unclear;
- uplift could be perceived as role-driven rather than capability-driven if the strategic case is not clear.

### Dependencies

- leadership support;
- workforce / classification review;
- clarity of mandate;
- role-holder alignment;
- agreement on reporting relationships.

### Customer or public value impact

Positive, primarily through stronger service foundations rather than direct front-end change.

### Organisational impact

Creates a defined internal capability using existing organisational foundations.

### Recommendation

**Preferred option, subject to validation.**

---

## Option B — Create a new Class/Band 6 role

### Description

Establish a new Customer Accounts and Information Services Lead role while retaining the existing Project Officer arrangement separately.

### Benefits

- clean role design;
- clearer distinction between project work and enduring service ownership;
- avoids forcing new responsibilities into an existing position.

### Costs or effort

- additional establishment process;
- potentially greater resourcing requirement;
- potential duplication with existing capability;
- longer pathway to implementation.

### Risks

- unnecessary organisational complexity;
- overlapping accountabilities;
- greater funding or headcount barrier.

### Dependencies

- workforce approval;
- funding;
- new role design;
- reporting model.

### Customer or public value impact

Potentially similar to Option A if implemented successfully.

### Organisational impact

Creates clearer structural separation but potentially with greater implementation cost.

### Recommendation

**Retain as fallback if amendment of the existing role is not organisationally appropriate.**

---

## Option C — Continue current arrangements

### Description

Continue customer account, information-quality and related activities through existing roles and project structures without formalising a lead capability.

### Benefits

- no immediate structural change;
- lowest short-term implementation effort;
- preserves current arrangements.

### Costs or effort

Low immediate cost, but continued coordination effort is likely.

### Risks

- fragmented priorities;
- unclear ownership;
- recurring task-based remediation;
- weak integration between related CX information capabilities;
- slower development of connected-interaction foundations.

### Dependencies

Continued informal coordination across teams and roles.

### Customer or public value impact

Existing improvements can continue, but system-wide benefits may be harder to realise.

### Organisational impact

Maintains flexibility but does not resolve the ownership question.

### Recommendation

**Do not prefer as the long-term model.**
