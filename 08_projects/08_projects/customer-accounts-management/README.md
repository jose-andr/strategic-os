# Customer Accounts Management

## Status

Active

## Purpose

Customer Accounts Management strengthens the quality, governance and usability of customer account information so that customer interactions can be supported by more reliable context across services and channels.

The work treats customer account quality as service infrastructure rather than only a data-clean-up activity.

Its strategic role is to improve current operational reliability while creating stronger foundations for connected customer interactions, analytics, automation and future AI-enabled services.

## Strategic question

> How do we create sufficiently trusted customer account information to improve current service delivery while enabling more connected, intelligent and automated customer interactions over time?

## Problem framing

Customer account quality can easily become a collection of recurring operational activities:

- duplicate identification and remediation;
- account hygiene;
- record correction;
- audits;
- reporting; and
- individual data-quality fixes.

Those activities remain necessary, but they do not address the full strategic problem.

If recurring quality problems are not understood and improved at source, poor customer information can continue to create:

- fragmented customer histories;
- repeated staff effort;
- weaker handover context;
- inconsistent CRM use;
- unreliable reporting and analytics;
- avoidable customer repetition;
- governance risk; and
- weaker foundations for automation and AI.

The opportunity is therefore to move from reactive record correction toward a managed Customer Accounts Management capability.

## Desired outcome

Establish a repeatable capability that:

- improves customer account quality;
- identifies and reduces recurring causes of poor-quality data;
- establishes practical ownership and quality controls;
- improves confidence in customer context;
- supports continuity between customer interactions;
- strengthens CRM and analytics reliability; and
- prepares customer information for appropriate future automation and AI use.

## Capability model

### 1. Customer account quality

Maintain practical approaches for:

- duplicate management;
- account hygiene;
- quality rules;
- record-quality monitoring; and
- prioritisation of material quality issues.

### 2. Quality assurance

Develop repeatable approaches for:

- audits;
- exception monitoring;
- quality measures;
- reporting;
- trend identification; and
- identification of systemic issues.

The objective is not simply to measure defects.

Measures should help determine where intervention creates meaningful customer, staff or organisational value.

### 3. Root-cause improvement

Move beyond correcting individual records by investigating:

- where quality problems originate;
- which processes repeatedly create them;
- whether system design contributes to them;
- whether staff guidance or service rules are unclear;
- whether ownership is ambiguous; and
- which interventions are likely to prevent recurrence.

Use continuous improvement to reduce recurring administration rather than institutionalising manual clean-up.

### 4. Governance and ownership

Translate broader governance requirements into practical operating rules for customer accounts.

Areas may include:

- ownership;
- quality expectations;
- appropriate access;
- information accuracy;
- privacy;
- records obligations;
- security; and
- escalation of material quality risks.

Organisational governance systems and approved platforms remain authoritative.

Strategic OS retains only safe interpretation, patterns and lessons.

### 5. Connected-context readiness

Determine what customer account information needs to be sufficiently trusted and available to support continuity across interactions.

This may include understanding:

- what context staff genuinely need;
- what information needs to persist across interactions;
- where repetition occurs;
- what information supports effective handover;
- what account information can safely support routing or personalisation; and
- what minimum information quality is required before automation is appropriate.

This does not assume that all customer information should be connected or exposed across systems.

Privacy, purpose, access and governance constraints remain part of the design problem.

## Business value

### Customer value

Better customer account information can contribute to:

- less unnecessary repetition;
- greater continuity between interactions;
- clearer recognition of previous activity where appropriate;
- more consistent service; and
- better handovers between channels or teams.

### Staff value

More reliable account information can contribute to:

- less manual reconstruction of customer context;
- reduced rework;
- clearer account histories;
- better-informed customer interactions; and
- more confidence in CRM information.

### Branch value

A managed capability creates:

- clearer ownership;
- an improvement roadmap;
- consistent quality practices;
- better visibility of systemic issues;
- prioritised improvement activity; and
- a stronger relationship between operational data quality and service outcomes.

### Organisational value

Trusted customer account information provides stronger foundations for:

- CRM improvement;
- service transformation;
- digital services;
- customer analytics;
- connected interactions;
- workflow automation; and
- appropriately governed AI-enabled capabilities.

## Viability argument

The viability case is not based on introducing another technology platform.

The capability can begin by strengthening and coordinating work that already exists:

- customer account quality activity;
- CRM improvement;
- data audits;
- duplicate management;
- continuous improvement;
- operational reporting; and
- governance practices.

This creates a pathway from existing operational activity toward a more managed service capability without requiring a large standalone transformation before value can be demonstrated.

The preferred approach is:

> Strengthen the operating capability first, demonstrate value through recurring problems, and introduce additional technology only where it removes meaningful friction or management burden.

## AI argumentation

Customer Accounts Management is not primarily an AI project.

Its relationship to AI is foundational.

AI-enabled systems can only reason over, retrieve or act on the context available to them. Poor-quality, duplicated or inconsistently governed customer information can therefore reduce the usefulness of AI or amplify existing information-quality problems.

The appropriate sequence is:

**Trusted customer accounts → reliable customer context → better connected interactions → stronger automation and AI readiness**

AI should not be positioned as the mechanism that makes poor customer information trustworthy.

Instead, Customer Accounts Management should establish the quality and governance conditions under which AI can potentially be used safely and effectively.

## AI readiness questions

Before proposing AI use, ask:

- Is the relevant customer information sufficiently accurate for the intended purpose?
- Are duplicates or fragmented identities likely to distort context?
- Is ownership clear?
- Is the information appropriately governed?
- Is access appropriate for the intended user or system?
- Is the information current enough for the decision being supported?
- Can the source be traced?
- Would automation amplify an existing quality problem?
- Is human review required?
- Does AI remove meaningful effort, or merely add another layer requiring management?

## Business value and AI decision rule

Do not pursue AI because customer account data exists.

Pursue an AI-enabled use case only when:

1. there is a clear customer, staff or organisational problem;
2. the required information is sufficiently trustworthy;
3. privacy and governance permit the use;
4. the AI capability materially improves the interaction or reduces recurring effort;
5. human oversight is appropriate to the risk; and
6. the solution removes more ongoing administration than it introduces.

## Strategic OS interpretation

The reusable principle emerging from this project is:

> Treat foundational information quality as strategic service infrastructure, not merely operational hygiene.

A useful capability progression is:

**Foundation → Capability → Experience → Intelligence**

For Customer Accounts Management:

**Trusted accounts → reliable customer context → connected experience → automation and AI potential**

This pattern should remain portable and employer-independent.

Project-specific organisational practices, raw data and controlled information remain in organisational systems of record.

## Relationship to related work

Customer Accounts Management is one part of a broader information foundation.

It connects particularly strongly with:

### Knowledge Management

Customer Accounts Management improves trust in information **about the customer**.

Knowledge Management improves trust in information **about services, processes and customer-facing guidance**.

Together they improve the context available to:

- frontline staff;
- digital channels;
- customer journeys;
- CRM;
- search;
- analytics;
- automation; and
- future AI-enabled assistance.

### Connected customer interactions

Connected interactions depend on both:

- trustworthy customer context; and
- trustworthy service information.

Neither capability alone creates a connected experience.

### Branch documentation

Branch documentation provides another layer of organisational knowledge: how work, decisions, responsibilities and reusable internal knowledge are structured and maintained.

The three projects therefore address different but related information domains:

- **Customer Accounts Management** — information about customers;
- **Knowledge Management** — information about services and customer support;
- **Branch Documentation Framework** — information about how the branch works.

Their relationship to AI should be assessed separately before identifying shared architecture or tooling.

## Current decisions

- Treat Customer Accounts Management as an enduring capability rather than a data-clean-up project.
- Lead with current business and customer value rather than AI.
- Position AI readiness as a downstream benefit of trusted information foundations.
- Prefer prevention and root-cause improvement over recurring manual correction.
- Keep organisational source data and controlled information in approved systems of record.
- Retain only safe summaries, patterns, interpretation and reusable learning in Strategic OS.
