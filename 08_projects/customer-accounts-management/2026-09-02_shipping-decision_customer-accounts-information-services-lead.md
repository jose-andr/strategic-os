# What Is Already Good Enough

## Problem

The current work provides a credible case that customer account quality, service information quality, information architecture and related continuous-improvement activity are becoming increasingly interconnected.

The problem can be expressed simply:

> Related CX information and account-quality activities risk remaining fragmented unless clearer service ownership is established.

This is sufficiently clear for socialisation.

## Strategic connection

The proposition connects directly with current work relating to:

- Channel Strategy Y2;
- customer account management;
- information architecture;
- knowledge-centred service;
- CRM adoption;
- Service Account improvements;
- connected customer interactions.

This is sufficiently established for an initial leadership discussion.

## Capability proposition

The proposed capability has a coherent core:

- customer account quality;
- service information quality;
- information architecture;
- continuous improvement;
- practical governance;
- connected-interactions readiness.

This is sufficiently defined to test.

## Preferred mechanism

The preferred mechanism is currently:

> evolve the existing Project Officer role into a Class/Band 6 Customer Accounts and Information Services Lead.

This should remain a **proposed implementation mechanism**, not the opening proposition.

# Minimum Socialisable Proposition

The proposition can be reduced to four points.

## 1. Emerging problem

Several CX priorities increasingly rely on the same customer account and service information foundations.

Today these activities risk being managed as related but separate pieces of work.

## 2. Opportunity

Create a managed CX capability that brings together:

- customer account quality;
- service information quality;
- information architecture;
- continuous improvement; and
- connected-interactions readiness.

## 3. Value

This would strengthen current operational quality while creating better foundations for CRM, digital, knowledge and connected customer interactions.

## 4. Question

> Do we think this has become an enduring CX capability that needs clearer ownership?

Only after that question receives support should the proposed role structure become the focus.

---

# Recommended Sponsor Conversation

## Objective

Determine whether there is sufficient leadership support to continue shaping the capability.

## Suggested opening

> I think several pieces of work we already have underway — customer account management, information architecture, service information and connected-channel work — are starting to converge into one capability question.

> Rather than treating them as separate projects, I think there may be value in creating clearer ownership for the customer account and information foundations that support our customer interactions.

## Core question

> Do you think this has become an enduring CX capability that needs clearer ownership, and is it worth shaping further?

## Supporting point

If useful:

> I have a possible operating model in mind using capability we already have, but I want to test the capability need first before locking in the structural answer.

## Do not lead with

- “I want to move from Band 5 to Band 6.”
- “I want these two roles reporting to me.”
- “I have designed a new team.”
- “This is the final operating model.”

Those may become relevant later, but they weaken the first strategic conversation.

---

# Expected Outcomes

The sponsor conversation should produce one of four signals.

## Signal 1 — Proceed

Leadership agrees that the capability need is real.

Next move:

Shape the operating model and organisational pathway.

## Signal 2 — Proceed with changes

Leadership agrees with the problem but challenges the proposed scope or ownership.

Next move:

Refine only the challenged areas.

## Signal 3 — Validate first

Leadership sees potential value but needs additional evidence.

Next move:

Identify the smallest evidence-gathering action required.

## Signal 4 — Stop or pause

Leadership does not currently see sufficient organisational need.

Next move:

Record the rationale and pause the opportunity.

Do not continue refining the proposal without a clear trigger.
