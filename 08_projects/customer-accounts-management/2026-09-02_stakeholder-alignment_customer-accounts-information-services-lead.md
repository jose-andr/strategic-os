# Stakeholder Alignment Assessment — Customer Accounts and Information Services Lead

## Work Or Decision

### Title

Customer Accounts and Information Services Lead

### Context

A proposal is being shaped to formalise an integrated CX capability covering customer account quality, service information quality, information architecture, continuous improvement and connected-interactions readiness.

The preferred implementation pathway is to evolve the existing Project Officer Customer Focus role into a Class/Band 6 lead role, subject to organisational review.

The strategic proposition is broader than the role change:

> Does this emerging CX capability now require explicit service ownership and integrated leadership?

### Decision Or Action Needed

Build sufficient stakeholder understanding and confidence to determine whether the capability should be formalised and, if so, what organisational pathway should be used.

### Current Stage

Shaping

### What Success Requires

Before a formal role or classification decision is sought:

- the capability problem is understood;
- the proposed scope is credible;
- organisational boundaries are clear;
- key delivery partners see how their responsibilities connect;
- the role mechanism is not mistaken for the strategic outcome;
- the formal decision pathway is understood.

# Alignment Assessment

## Overall Alignment

Unclear

The strategic need appears aligned with existing CX priorities, but individual stakeholder support, organisational feasibility and role-boundary agreement have not yet been validated.

## What Is Already Aligned?

The existing work provides evidence of alignment around:

- improving customer account quality;
- improving service information quality;
- strengthening information architecture;
- reducing recurring quality problems at source;
- improving CRM reliability;
- preparing for connected customer interactions;
- creating stronger information foundations.

These themes already exist across current CX work.

## What Is Not Yet Aligned?

The following remain open:

- whether these activities should become one formally managed capability;
- whether the existing Project Officer role is the right mechanism;
- whether Class/Band 6 is the appropriate classification;
- whether the Information Officer and Customer Data Officer should report through this capability;
- where the boundary sits between CX ownership and enterprise governance;
- how much remit the capability should initially carry.

## Where Is There Uncertainty?

Key uncertainties are:

- the formal role approval pathway;
- classification requirements;
- current reporting constraints;
- the willingness of adjacent capability owners to adopt the proposed boundaries;
- whether service information and customer account quality should be integrated immediately or progressively;
- the measures that would demonstrate value.

## Where Is There Potential Resistance?

No stakeholder resistance has been validated.

Potential concerns to test, rather than assume, include:

- perceived duplication of existing governance responsibilities;
- concern that the proposed role is too broad;
- concern that the proposal is primarily a classification uplift;
- concern about changing reporting arrangements;
- uncertainty about ownership between CX, CRM, data and knowledge functions;
- concern about introducing additional governance or process overhead.

These are hypotheses for engagement, not statements about stakeholder views.

## Confidence Level

Medium–low confidence

Reason:

The capability rationale is well developed, but stakeholder positions have not yet been directly validated.

## Formal Decision Pathway

To validate.

Likely elements include:

1. Customer Focus and Strategy sponsorship;
2. CX leadership endorsement;
3. workforce / classification assessment;
4. any required organisational approval for role structure and reporting changes.

## Informal Influence Pathway

Confidence is likely to be shaped by whether adjacent capability owners believe the proposal:

- solves a real operational problem;
- respects existing ownership;
- reduces fragmentation;
- creates practical value;
- avoids unnecessary bureaucracy.

Early conversations with CRM, information, knowledge and governance partners can therefore improve the proposal before formal approval.

## Implementation Pathway

If approved, implementation will require participation from:

- Information Officer;
- Customer Data Officer;
- CRM / Salesforce partners;

- governance partners;

- service owners affected by customer account problems;
- Digital / MVGA;
- privacy;
- records;
- information security;
- analytics / VoC partners.

Do not expand consultation automatically. Include them where their input materially changes the decision or operating model.

# Core Narrative

## One-Line Message

> We have an opportunity to turn related customer account and service information activities into a managed CX capability that improves current service quality and creates stronger foundations for connected customer interactions.

## Why This Matters

Customer accounts, service information, CRM quality, knowledge and information architecture increasingly depend on one another.

Without clearer ownership, these activities risk remaining fragmented and reactive.

## What Changes

The proposed model would shift from:

> related tasks and individual projects

toward:

> an integrated internal service capability with clearer ownership, standards, priorities, measures and continuous improvement.

## What Is Needed Now

Agreement that the capability problem is valid and worth shaping further.

The immediate ask should not be final approval of every structural detail.

## What Is Still Open

The following should remain genuinely open to stakeholder input:

- final role title;
- final remit;
- classification;
- reporting arrangements;
- detailed decision rights;
- implementation sequence;
- service measures;
- whether all proposed capability elements begin at the same time.

## Interpretation

The strongest engagement strategy is to **socialise the capability before socialising the organisational solution**.

The proposal is more likely to receive useful challenge and support if stakeholders are invited to shape:

- whether the capability is needed;
- what it should own;
- what it should not own;
- what organisational mechanism best supports it.

This preserves genuine participation and reduces the risk that stakeholder engagement is experienced as endorsement-seeking for a predetermined role outcome.
